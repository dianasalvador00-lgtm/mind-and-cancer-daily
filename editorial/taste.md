# Reader profile

Mind and Cancer Daily is written for a smart researcher who is not an expert in every neighbouring
field: an oncologist, a neuroscientist and a materials scientist should be able to read the same
page. Tune this file freely. The daily run reads it every morning and folds "More like this" and
"Less like this" feedback into it. This repository is public, so keep personal details out of it.

## Who

- A biomedical PhD researcher working on nanomedicine and phototherapy in cancer, curious about
  medical science in general, with a particular interest in cancer and in the mind.
- Reads for: what is new and credible, what it means, how strong the evidence is, and what could be
  borrowed from a neighbouring field.

## Areas and target share of each issue

| Area | Share | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| cancer | 35% | biology, tumour microenvironment, therapies, immunotherapy, early detection, trials |
| mind | 35% | neuroscience, psychiatry, psychology, cognition, neurodegeneration, behaviour |
| rare | 15% | gene and cell therapy, diagnosis, natural history, registries |
| curiosity | 10% | odd but real findings, well sourced |
| other | 5% | anything else in medicine that is genuinely notable |

The shares are targets over a week, not quotas for one day. `config/topics.json` has the same
numbers for the sweep.

## The boosted niche (inside cancer)

Nanoparticle design and delivery into dense-stroma tumours, photodynamic and photothermal therapy,
photosensitizers, near-infrared agents, upconversion, light dosimetry, pancreatic cancer (PDAC) and
its microenvironment, combinations with chemotherapy and immunotherapy, organoids and KPC mice,
characterization (DLS, TEM, zeta potential, imaging), translation and regulation, funding calls and
good scientific practice. A strong item here earns a place even on a day when the rest is quiet,
and a Learn one thing or Method of the day from this niche is welcome about once a week.

## What earns a place

- A primary result with numbers: sample size, effect size, a clear comparison.
- A trial that changes practice or a first-in-human result, with phase and status.
- A method or analysis approach that transfers across fields.
- A rare disease first, or a rare disease result that shows a way to study small populations.
- A retraction, correction or data problem that readers should know about.
- Open datasets and tools that other labs can reuse.
- A curiosity that is real, sourced and teaches something.

## What does not

- Press-release hype and wellness claims with no study behind them.
- Single-animal or single-cell-line headlines presented as human findings (cover them only when they
  are truly strong, and say what they did not show).
- Anything with no accessible primary source that the run could read.
- Old news, unless it was missed and still matters.
- Medical advice of any kind.

## Evidence habits

Label what a study is (preprint, peer reviewed, review, trial with phase and status, in vitro,
organoid, mouse model and which, human data, computational only). Give sample size and the main
limitation. Say what it did not show. Flag funding or conflicts when the source states them.

## Jobs

Worldwide, with higher pay preferred. Roles: PhD, postdoc, fellowship, staff scientist and industry
scientist, in oncology, neuroscience, nanomedicine, drug delivery, biomedical engineering and
related fields. A salary is shown only when the posting states it, never estimated. This section
will be sharpened later with the owner's skills; add generic skill keywords to `config/topics.json`.

## Tone

Plain words, short sentences, concrete nouns. Define jargon from neighbouring fields. Start every
item with one plain sentence and fold the depth under Details. No hype words (breakthrough, cure,
revolutionary, game-changing, miracle, groundbreaking, unprecedented).

## Public and private

The site is public. Nothing about the owner's institution, lab, unpublished work or hypotheses goes
in any public text. A personal angle goes in `forYou`, which is hidden on the page but is visible in
the page source, so write it generically ("relevant to nanoparticle penetration of dense stroma")
with no unpublished details.
